# Fiqh files keep to points the four Sunni schools agree on. Where they differ, the clue names the school.

## 100
The obligatory alms, the third pillar of Islam. :: Zakat || H: Sahih al-Bukhari

The usual rate of zakat on money and trade goods. :: 2.5% (one fortieth) || H: Sunan Abi Dawud
The least wealth on which zakat is due. :: Nisab || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
The wealth must be held for this long before zakat is due. :: One lunar year (hawl) || H: Sunan Abi Dawud
The verse that names the eight kinds of people who receive zakat. :: Qur'an 9:60 || H: Qur'an 9:60
The first two kinds of zakat recipients in that verse. :: The poor and the needy (fuqara' and masakin) || H: Qur'an 9:60
Zakat is often paired in the Qur'an with this. :: Prayer (salah) || H: Qur'an 2:43
The caliph who fought those who refused to pay zakat. :: Our master Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
The charity paid at the end of Ramadan. :: Zakat al-fitr || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
Zakat is paid on gold and this metal. :: Silver || H: Sahih Muslim
Zakat is paid on these animals that graze freely. :: Camels, cattle, sheep and goats || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
Zakat is paid on these crops at harvest. :: Grains and fruits (such as dates) || H: Qur'an 6:141
Zakat on crops is due on the day of this. :: Harvest || H: Qur'an 6:141
A person's own home is this, for zakat. :: Not zakatable || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
A person's own riding animal or car is this, for zakat. :: Not zakatable || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
Our Beloved Master ﷺ sent this Companion to Yemen to teach that zakat is taken from the rich and given to the poor. :: Mu'adh ibn Jabal (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
Zakat cannot be given to these close relatives one must support, by agreement. :: Parents and children || H: al-Mughni (Ibn Qudamah)
Zakat may not be given to the family of this person. :: Our Beloved Prophet ﷺ (Banu Hashim) || H: Sahih Muslim
The intention to pay zakat is this. :: A condition || H: Nur al-Idah; Matn Abi Shuja'

## 300
In the Hanafi school, zakat on women's gold jewellery is this. :: Due || H: Nur al-Idah; al-Hidayah
In the Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali schools, zakat on women's permitted jewellery is this. :: Not due (by the main view) || H: Matn Abi Shuja'; al-Risalah; Zad al-Mustaqni'
In the Hanafi school, the "nisab" for zakat al-fitr and qurbani is reached by owning this beyond needs. :: The value of the nisab || H: Nur al-Idah (al-Shurunbulali)
In the Hanafi school, gold and silver are added together to reach nisab by this. :: Value || H: al-Hidayah (al-Marghinani)
In the Shafi'i school, gold and silver are added together like this. :: They are not added together || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
Trade goods are valued for zakat at this point. :: At the end of the year, at market value || H: Nur al-Idah; Matn Abi Shuja'
Zakat on a debt owed to you that you expect to be paid, in the Hanafi school. :: Due when it is received, for past years || H: al-Hidayah (al-Marghinani)
In the Hanafi school, zakat on crops is due on these, whatever the amount. :: All that the land produces || H: al-Hidayah (al-Marghinani)
In the Shafi'i school, zakat on crops is due on these. :: Staple foods that can be stored, and dates and raisins || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
Paying zakat before its year has passed is allowed by the majority, called this. :: Ta'jil al-zakat || H: Sunan Abi Dawud; Sunan al-Tirmidhi
The Companion who asked our Beloved Prophet ﷺ to pay his zakat early. :: Our master al-'Abbas (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sunan Abi Dawud
In the Hanafi school, zakat may be given to one recipient type only. :: Yes, it may || H: al-Hidayah (al-Marghinani)
In the Shafi'i school, zakat should be shared among this many recipient types when present. :: All eight (all present) || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
In the Hanafi school, moving zakat to another town is disliked, except to these. :: Needy relatives or a more needy people || H: Nur al-Idah (al-Shurunbulali)
In the Hanafi school, the poor person (faqir) owns this. :: Less than the nisab || H: Nur al-Idah (al-Shurunbulali)
In the Hanafi school, the needy (miskin) owns this. :: Nothing || H: Nur al-Idah (al-Shurunbulali)
In the Shafi'i school, the faqir is worse off than this. :: The miskin || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
In the Shafi'i school, a wife may give her zakat to this person if he is poor. :: Her husband || H: Matn Abi Shuja'; Sahih al-Bukhari
The wife of Ibn Mas'ud (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِمَا) who asked about giving charity to her husband. :: Zaynab (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
In the Hanafi, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools, zakat on livestock is due only if they do this for most of the year. :: Graze freely (sa'imah) || H: al-Hidayah; Matn Abi Shuja'
