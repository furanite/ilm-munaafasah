# Fiqh files keep to points the four Sunni schools agree on. Where they differ, the clue names the school.

## 100
The science of inheritance shares. :: 'Ilm al-Fara'id (al-Mirath) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The verses of inheritance are mainly in this surah. :: Surah al-Nisa' || H: Qur'an 4:11-12, 4:176
The person who has died and left wealth. :: Muwarrith (the deceased) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A person who inherits. :: Warith (heir) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The wealth left behind. :: Tarikah (estate) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The first thing paid from the estate. :: Funeral costs || H: al-Sirajiyyah
After funeral costs, this is paid before any shares. :: Debts || H: Qur'an 4:11; al-Sirajiyyah
A bequest may be up to this much of the estate. :: One third || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
A son receives this compared with a daughter. :: Twice the share || H: Qur'an 4:11
A husband with no children of his wife receives this share. :: One half || H: Qur'an 4:12
A husband when his wife has children receives this. :: One quarter || H: Qur'an 4:12
A wife when her husband has no children receives this. :: One quarter || H: Qur'an 4:12
A wife when her husband has children receives this. :: One eighth || H: Qur'an 4:12
A single daughter, with no son, receives this. :: One half || H: Qur'an 4:11
Two or more daughters, with no son, share this. :: Two thirds || H: Qur'an 4:11
Each parent with the deceased's children receives this. :: One sixth || H: Qur'an 4:11
The mother with no children or siblings of the deceased receives this. :: One third || H: Qur'an 4:11
The six fixed shares in the Qur'an include 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 2/3, 1/3 and this. :: 1/6 || H: Qur'an 4:11-12, 4:176; al-Sirajiyyah
Our Beloved Prophet ﷺ said learn the fara'id, for it is half of this (paraphrased). :: Knowledge || H?: Sunan Ibn Majah; grade to be checked
A Muslim does not inherit from a non-Muslim, nor this. :: A non-Muslim from a Muslim || H: Sahih al-Bukhari

## 200
Heirs with fixed shares in the Qur'an. :: Ashab al-furud || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Heirs who take what is left after the fixed shares. :: 'Asabah (residuaries) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
Our Beloved Prophet ﷺ said give the shares to their people, and what is left goes to this. :: The nearest male relative || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
A person who kills the one he would inherit from does this. :: Does not inherit || H: Sunan al-Tirmidhi; Muwatta' Malik
The three reasons for inheriting: blood, marriage and this. :: Wala' (clientship from freeing a slave) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The three things that block inheritance: killing, difference of religion and this. :: Slavery || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Being blocked from inheriting by a nearer heir. :: Hajb || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Total blocking is called this. :: Hajb hirman || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Partial blocking, lowering a share, is called this. :: Hajb nuqsan || H: al-Sirajiyyah
These six heirs can never be totally blocked. :: Husband, wife, son, daughter, father, mother || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A son blocks this sibling from inheriting. :: Brothers and sisters || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A father blocks these from inheriting. :: Brothers, sisters and the grandfather || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The mother's share is lowered from 1/3 to 1/6 by this many siblings. :: Two or more || H: Qur'an 4:11
A person who dies with no parent and no child. :: Kalalah || H: Qur'an 4:12; 4:176
The last verse of Surah al-Nisa' is about this. :: Kalalah || H: Qur'an 4:176
Uterine (maternal) siblings share in this way. :: Equally, male and female || H: Qur'an 4:12
One uterine sibling receives this share. :: One sixth || H: Qur'an 4:12
Two or more uterine siblings share this. :: One third || H: Qur'an 4:12
The grandmother receives this share. :: One sixth || H: Sunan Abi Dawud
The Companion who gave the grandmother 1/6 after al-Mughirah and Muhammad ibn Maslamah testified. :: Our master Abu Bakr (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sunan Abi Dawud; Muwatta' Malik

## 300
The famous Hanafi text on inheritance by Siraj al-Din al-Sajawandi رحمة الله عليه. :: al-Sirajiyyah || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The famous Shafi'i poem on inheritance by al-Rahbi رحمة الله عليه. :: al-Rahbiyyah || H: al-Rahbiyyah
When the shares add up to more than the whole, they are all reduced. This is called this. :: 'Awl || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The caliph who first ruled by 'awl. :: Our master 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: al-Sunan al-Kubra (al-Bayhaqi)
The Companion who did not hold the view of 'awl. :: 'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِمَا) || H: al-Sunan al-Kubra (al-Bayhaqi)
When shares add up to less than the whole and there is no residuary, the rest is given back. This is called this. :: Radd || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In the Hanafi school, radd goes to these heirs. :: The share-holders, except husband and wife || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Relatives who are neither share-holders nor residuaries. :: Dhawu al-arham || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In the Hanafi and Hanbali schools, dhawu al-arham inherit when this. :: There is no share-holder (for radd) or residuary || H: al-Sirajiyyah; Zad al-Mustaqni'
In the early Shafi'i and Maliki view, when no heir is left the estate goes to this. :: The public treasury (bayt al-mal) || H: Matn Abi Shuja'; al-Risalah
The famous case of the "two 'Umariyyahs" concerns a spouse and these. :: Both parents || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In the 'Umariyyatan, the mother gets this. :: One third of what remains || H: al-Sirajiyyah; Muwatta' Malik
The case of the "common donkey" (al-Himariyyah) concerns full brothers and these. :: Uterine brothers || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In al-Himariyyah, our master 'Umar (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) finally ruled this, in one report. :: Full and uterine brothers share the third || H: al-Sunan al-Kubra (al-Bayhaqi)
The Hanafi school in al-Himariyyah rules this. :: Full brothers get nothing || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The Shafi'i and Maliki schools in al-Himariyyah rule this. :: Full brothers share with the uterine || H: Matn Abi Shuja'; al-Risalah
The case called "al-Minbariyyah" was answered by our master 'Ali (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) while on this. :: The pulpit || H: al-Sunan al-Kubra (al-Bayhaqi)
In al-Minbariyyah, the wife's 1/8 became this. :: 1/9 (3/27) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base number of a problem in fara'id is called this. :: Asl al-mas'alah || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The possible bases include 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and this. :: 24 || H: al-Sirajiyyah

## 400
In the Hanafi school, the grandfather with brothers is treated like this. :: The father (he blocks the brothers) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In the Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali schools, the grandfather with brothers does this. :: Shares with them || H: Matn Abi Shuja'; al-Risalah
The Hanafi view on the grandfather follows this Companion. :: Our master Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari (ta'liq)
The view of sharing with brothers follows this Companion. :: Zayd ibn Thabit (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: al-Sunan al-Kubra (al-Bayhaqi)
Our Beloved Master ﷺ said the most knowledgeable of fara'id among them is this Companion (paraphrased). :: Zayd ibn Thabit (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sunan al-Tirmidhi
Sisters with daughters become this. :: Residuaries ('asabah ma'a al-ghayr) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
The Companion who ruled that a sister with a daughter and a son's daughter takes the rest. :: 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
In that case, the son's daughter receives this, completing the two thirds. :: One sixth || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
A son's daughter with a daughter receives this. :: One sixth || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
The residuary by himself, such as a son. :: 'Asabah bi-nafsih || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A female made residuary by a male of the same level, such as a daughter with a son. :: 'Asabah bil-ghayr || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The residuaries by themselves are in four classes: sons, fathers, brothers and these. :: Uncles || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The rule among residuaries: the nearer class first, then the nearer degree, then this. :: Strength of tie (full before half) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A full brother blocks this relative. :: A paternal half-brother || H: al-Sirajiyyah
Our Beloved Prophet ﷺ said full siblings inherit, not half-siblings by the father, as narrated by this Companion. :: Our master 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Sunan al-Tirmidhi
An unborn child's share is kept aside; the Hanafi fatwa keeps the share of this many sons. :: One || H: al-Sirajiyyah
A missing person (mafqud) is treated as alive for his own wealth until this. :: A judge rules him dead || H: al-Sirajiyyah
People who die together with no knowledge of who died first do this. :: Do not inherit from each other || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The share of a hermaphrodite (khuntha mushkil) in the Hanafi school. :: The lesser of the two shares || H: al-Sirajiyyah
An apostate's wealth gained before apostasy goes to his Muslim heirs in this school. :: The Hanafi school || H: al-Sirajiyyah

## 500
The famous case of "al-Akdariyyah" is a problem with a husband, mother, grandfather and this. :: A sister || H: al-Sirajiyyah; Matn Abi Shuja'
In al-Akdariyyah, the base of the problem rises by 'awl from 6 to this. :: 9 || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
The final base of al-Akdariyyah after correction. :: 27 || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
The case of al-Akdariyyah is linked to this Companion's method. :: Zayd ibn Thabit (رِضْوَانُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ) || H: Matn Abi Shuja'
The case of "al-Mushtarakah" is another name for this case. :: al-Himariyyah || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The "lucky" relative (al-akh al-mubarak) is one who makes a female relative inherit. Who is the "unlucky" one (al-akh al-mash'um)? :: One who blocks a female who would inherit without him || H: al-Rahbiyyah (commentaries)
Making the shares into whole numbers is called this. :: Tashih || H: al-Sirajiyyah
When an heir dies before the estate is divided. :: Munasakhah || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base 24 is used when there is 1/8 with this share. :: 1/6 or 2/3 || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base 12 is used when there is 1/4 with this share. :: 1/6, 1/3 or 2/3 || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base 6 can rise by 'awl up to this number. :: 10 || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base 12 can rise by 'awl to 13, 15 or this. :: 17 || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The base 24 can rise by 'awl only to this. :: 27 || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The case of al-Kharqa' (mother, grandfather and sister) is also called al-Mukhammasah because this many Companions differed on it. :: 5 || H?: al-Sirajiyyah; to be checked
The commentary on al-Sirajiyyah by al-Sayyid al-Sharif al-Jurjani رحمة الله عليه. :: al-Sharifiyyah || H: al-Sharifiyyah
The Maliki chapter on inheritance in Ibn Abi Zayd's Risalah is called this. :: Bab al-Fara'id || H: al-Risalah (Ibn Abi Zayd)
The commentary on al-Rahbiyyah by Sibt al-Maridini رحمة الله عليه. :: Sharh al-Rahbiyyah || H?: Title to be checked
Inheritance shares are fixed by the heirs alive at this moment. :: The death of the deceased || H: al-Sirajiyyah
In the Hanafi school, a female residuary through the son, like a son's daughter, is pulled up by this relative. :: A son's son of the same level (or lower, when needed) || H: al-Sirajiyyah
The Hanafi principle "the nearer blocks the farther" in residuaries is from this hadith. :: "Give the shares to their people; the rest is for the nearest male" || H: Sahih al-Bukhari
